# React Chess Arena: Home Page, Two Players and Computer Mode

An offline chess game built with React (Vite). No chess library is used: all rules are written in `src/chess/engine.js`, and the computer opponent in `src/chess/ai.js`.

## Pages
- **Home:** hero section, feature cards and how-to-play steps
- **Game Setup:** choose Two Players or vs Computer, difficulty (Easy / Medium / Hard), your colour and time per player (3, 5, 10 or 15 minutes)
- **Game:** the chessboard, clocks, move list and controls (Undo, Restart, Menu)

## Features
- 8x8 board with pieces in their starting positions
- Legal move validation for all pieces, including castling, en passant and pawn promotion
- Illegal moves are blocked and the player is notified
- Alternating turns (one move per turn)
- Check and checkmate detection with on-screen notifications (also stalemate and insufficient-material draws)
- Countdown timer for each player (10 minutes); only the player to move loses time, and a player who runs out of time loses
- Move list in standard algebraic notation (e4, Nf3, exd5, O-O, e8=Q+, Qh5#)
- Optional extras: undo last move, highlight of legal moves for the selected piece, captured pieces for each player, last-move and king-in-check highlights

## Project structure
- `src/chess/engine.js`: pure functions for move generation, check detection, status and notation
- `src/chess/ai.js`: computer player (negamax search with alpha-beta pruning)
- `src/App.jsx`: switches between Home, Setup and Game screens
- `src/components/`: `Home.jsx`, `Setup.jsx`, `Game.jsx` (board UI and game state with `useState`, `useEffect`, `useMemo`)
- `src/index.css`: styling

## Decisions made during the enhancement process
- **Computer opponent:** negamax with alpha-beta pruning, capture-first move ordering and a short capture-only search to avoid obvious blunders. Evaluation counts material plus small positional bonuses. Easy plays a random move 35% of the time and looks 1 move ahead, Medium looks 2 moves ahead, Hard looks 3.
- **AI runs after a short delay:** a `setTimeout` lets the player's move render first, and the banner shows "computer thinking".
- **Undo vs computer:** undoes two moves (the computer's reply and yours) so it is your turn again.
- **Board flips** when you play Black against the computer.
- **Screens without a router:** one `screen` state switches Home, Setup and Game, keeping the app small.
- **Verified AI legality:** self-play games at every level produced only legal moves.
- **Engine separate from UI:** the rules are pure functions that take a state and return a new one, which makes the game easy to undo (just drop the last position) and easy to test.
- **Pseudo-legal then filter:** moves are generated by piece movement rules first, then any move that leaves the mover's own king attacked is removed. This handles pins and check naturally.
- **Verified with perft:** the move generator was checked against standard perft node counts (start position depth 4 = 197,281; Kiwipete depth 3 = 97,862; plus en passant and promotion test positions), and all matched.
- **Check/checkmate/stalemate:** no legal moves means checkmate if the king is attacked, otherwise stalemate.
- **Notation:** disambiguation (Nbd7, R1e2) and `+` / `#` suffixes are added when needed.
- **Timers:** a one-second `setInterval` inside `useEffect` that depends on whose turn it is, so the inactive player's clock is paused automatically.
- **Undo:** restores the board and move list; clocks are not restored (time already used stays used).
- **Pieces:** Unicode chess glyphs styled with CSS, so no image files are needed.
